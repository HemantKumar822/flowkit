# Product brief

Adapted from BMAD Method's product brief template, copyright (c) 2025 BMad Code, LLC and contributors. MIT; [full notice](../../third-party/bmad/LICENSE). Exact source and changes: [provenance](../../third-party/sources.json).

Use for an unclear or new product, not every feature. Fill from actual evidence and delegated decisions; mark assumptions and unresolved questions. Remove sections that add no value.

## Product in one paragraph
What are we building, for whom, and what useful outcome does it enable?

## Problem and current alternatives
Describe the user's actual situation, what they do today, the friction and the evidence. Separate observed facts from assumptions. Do not invent interviews, market demand or savings.

## Proposed experience
Describe the main user journey and the result. Explain why this scope is useful before specifying frameworks.

## Why this approach
Compare plausible alternatives. Explain the tradeoff and what is distinctive without manufacturing a technical advantage.

## Users and success
Identify primary users, their job and observable success. Record business goals only when known. Label proposed success measures as targets, not achieved results.

## First useful version
State what is included, what is deliberately excluded, constraints, dependencies and the smallest end-to-end flow worth building.

## Decisions and next step
Record consequential open questions, who owns them, what can proceed under existing authority, and the next acceptance task. Link the project goal/decisions instead of copying contradictory state.
